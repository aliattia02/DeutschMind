📊Plural Patterns Overview

German has 5 main plural patterns. Unlike English (just add -s), German plural formation depends on the word's gender, ending, and origin.

Good news: The plural article is ALWAYS die (regardless of the singular gender)!

🎨 The 5 Plural Patterns

🔵 Pattern 1: -e or -¨e

Add -e or -¨e (with umlaut on a/o/u)

der Tisch → die Tische der Stuhl → die Stühle das Problem → die Probleme

🟢 Pattern 2: -en or -n

Add -en or -n (most feminine nouns)

die Frau → die Frauen die Blume → die Blumen die Tür → die Türen

🟠 Pattern 3: -er or -¨er

Add -er or -¨er (mainly neuter and some masculine)

das Kind → die Kinder das Buch → die Bücher der Mann → die Männer

🟣 Pattern 4: -s

Add -s (mostly foreign/borrowed words)

das Auto → die Autos das Hotel → die Hotels das Foto → die Fotos

🔴 Pattern 5: No Change

Stays the same (words ending in -er, -el, -en)

der Lehrer → die Lehrer der Apfel → die Äpfel das Fenster → die Fenster
💡 Important Rules:
• ALL plurals use die as the article
• Umlaut (¨) changes a→ä, o→ö, u→ü in many plurals
• You MUST memorize the plural with each noun!
• Pattern -en/-n is most common (about 40% of all nouns)

📈 Quick Statistics

Pattern Frequency Typical Genders
-e / -¨e ~30% Masculine, Neuter
-en / -n ~40% Mostly Feminine
-er / -¨er ~15% Neuter, some Masculine

No change ~10% Masculine, Neuter

🔵Pattern: -e / -¨e

This pattern adds -e to the singular form. Many words also add an umlaut (¨) on the vowel a, o, or u.

Common with: Many masculine and some neuter one-syllable nouns

📘 Simple -e (no umlaut)

🔊
der Tisch → die Tische
the table → the tables
🔊
der Hund → die Hunde
the dog → the dogs
🔊
das Problem → die Probleme
the problem → the problems
🔊
der Tag → die Tage
the day → the days
🔊
das Jahr → die Jahre
the year → the years
🔊
der Beruf → die Berufe
the profession → the professions

📗 With Umlaut: -¨e

🔊
der Stuhl → die Stühle
the chair → the chairs
🔊
die Stadt → die Städte
the city → the cities
🔊
die Hand → die Hände
the hand → the hands
🔊
der Sohn → die Söhne
the son → the sons
🔊
der Baum → die Bäume
the tree → the trees
🔊
die Wand → die Wände
the wall → the walls
🎯 When to use umlaut?
• One-syllable words with a, o, u usually get umlaut: Ball → Bälle
• Multi-syllable words usually don't: Tisch → Tische
• Unfortunately, you must memorize each word!

🧠 Memory Trick for Umlaut

"Short and Strong needs the ¨ song!"

One-syllable masculine nouns often get umlaut + e

🟢Pattern: -en / -n

This is the MOST COMMON plural pattern (~40% of all nouns). It's used mostly for feminine nouns.

If the word already ends in -e, just add -n. Otherwise, add -en.

📕 Adding -en

🔊
die Frau → die Frauen
the woman → the women
🔊
die Tür → die Türen
the door → the doors
🔊
die Zeit → die Zeiten
the time → the times
🔊
die Zahl → die Zahlen
the number → the numbers
🔊
die Uhr → die Uhren
the clock → the clocks
🔊
die Frage → die Fragen
the question → the questions

📙 Just adding -n (words ending in -e)

🔊
die Blume → die Blumen
the flower → the flowers
🔊
die Lampe → die Lampen
the lamp → the lamps
🔊
die Schule → die Schulen
the school → the schools
🔊
die Katze → die Katzen
the cat → the cats
🔊
die Minute → die Minuten
the minute → the minutes
🔊
die Seite → die Seiten
the page/side → the pages/sides

📓 Masculine nouns with -en (less common)

🔊
der Mensch → die Menschen
the person → the people
🔊
der Student → die Studenten
the student → the students
🔊
der Herr → die Herren
the gentleman → the gentlemen
💡 Easy Rule:
• Most feminine nouns use this pattern
• Words ending in -e just add -n
• Words ending in -ung, -heit, -keit, -schaft, -tion always add -en
• This pattern NEVER uses umlaut!

🧠 Memory Trick

"Feminine words are gentle, just add -en!"

If it's feminine and you're not sure, try -en/-n first!

🟠Pattern: -er / -¨er

This pattern adds -er and almost always adds an umlaut if the word has a, o, or u.

Common with: Many neuter one-syllable nouns and some masculine nouns

📒 Simple -er (no umlaut possible)

🔊
das Ei → die Eier
the egg → the eggs
🔊
das Bild → die Bilder
the picture → the pictures
🔊
das Lied → die Lieder
the song → the songs

📔 With Umlaut: -¨er (most common)

🔊
das Kind → die Kinder
the child → the children
🔊
das Buch → die Bücher
the book → the books
🔊
das Haus → die Häuser
the house → the houses
🔊
das Land → die Länder
the country → the countries
🔊
der Mann → die Männer
the man → the men
🔊
das Wort → die Wörter
the word → the words
🔊
das Dorf → die Dörfer
the village → the villages
🔊
das Glas → die Gläser
the glass → the glasses
💡 Pattern Rules:
• Mainly one-syllable neuter nouns: das Buch → die Bücher
• Umlaut is almost always added if there's a, o, or u
• This is the "super plural" - both ending AND umlaut!
• Exception: das Kind (no i-umlaut exists!)

🧠 Memory Trick

"-ER is the super power, adds umlaut in the same hour!"

Neuter one-syllable words often get -er + umlaut

🟣Pattern: -s & No Change

These are the two easiest patterns! Pattern -s is mostly for foreign/borrowed words, and no change is for words ending in -er, -el, -en.

🟣 Pattern -s (Foreign Words)

🔊
das Auto → die Autos
the car → the cars
🔊
das Hotel → die Hotels
the hotel → the hotels
🔊
das Foto → die Fotos
the photo → the photos
🔊
das Kino → die Kinos
the cinema → the cinemas
🔊
das Baby → die Babys
the baby → the babies
🔊
das Sofa → die Sofas
the sofa → the sofas

🔴 Pattern: No Change

🔊
der Lehrer → die Lehrer
the teacher → the teachers
🔊
das Fenster → die Fenster
the window → the windows
🔊
der Schüler → die Schüler
the student → the students
🔊
das Zimmer → die Zimmer
the room → the rooms
🔊
der Onkel → die Onkel
the uncle → the uncles
🔊
der Apfel → die Äpfel
the apple → the apples
💡 Easy Recognition:
• -s: Foreign words, especially ending in -o, -a, -i, -u
• No change: Words ending in -er, -el, -en, -chen, -lein
• Exception: Some "no change" words add umlaut: Apfel → Äpfel
• Article ALWAYS changes: der/das → die

🧠 Memory Tricks

Pattern -s: "Foreign words keep it simple, just add -s!"

No change: "Words with -er stay the same, just change the name!" (article)
